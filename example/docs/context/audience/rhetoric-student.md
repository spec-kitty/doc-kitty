# The Rhetoric Student

This reader is new to Aristotle's *Rhetoric* and approaches it as a text to be
studied, not skimmed. They may be encountering classical rhetorical theory for
the first time as part of a course in communication, argumentation, or classics,
or they may be reading independently to understand where later persuasion theory
comes from.

What they need most is a version of the text that does not assume prior
familiarity: unfamiliar terms glossed in place, footnotes that read as part of
the argument rather than as scholarly clutter, and enough structural signposting
(book, chapter, section) to keep their bearings across a long, dense work. They
are patient with the original argument's pace, but impatient with anything that
makes the text harder to follow than the ideas already are.

They measure a converted showcase page as successful when they can read a
chapter start to finish, resolve every unfamiliar term without leaving the page,
and come away able to summarise the chapter's argument in their own words.
