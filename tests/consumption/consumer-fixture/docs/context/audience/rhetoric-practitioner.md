# The Rhetoric Practitioner

This reader already knows why persuasion matters; they come to Aristotle's
*Rhetoric* looking for technique they can put to work, not a survey of classical
philosophy. They might be preparing a speech, coaching a debate team, or
writing professionally, and they treat the text as a working reference rather
than a cover-to-cover read.

What they need most is a primary text they can navigate non-linearly: reliable
cross-references between related passages, footnotes that connect a classical
example to its modern analogue, and enough structural clarity to jump straight
to the chapter that covers the technique they are after. They are less
concerned with the text's historical context than with whether a given passage
still holds up as usable guidance.

They measure a converted showcase page as successful when they can find the
passage that names a specific persuasive technique, understand it well enough
to apply it, and trace it back to the original wording if they need to cite or
quote it.
